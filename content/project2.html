What we learned from the comparisons

There is no overall winner established by these papers. The systems answer different questions and use different data. Together, they suggest four practical lessons for our mobile coach.

  1. Start with an explainable baseline. Pose Trainer gives traceable geometric rules, and the isometric study shows simple angle features can be competitive. Compare such a baseline before assuming an image classifier or larger model is necessary. [3] [2]
  2. Retain movement when the fault depends on it. DTW, AIFit, SiTrEx, and the squat classifier evaluate sequences or repetition summaries. A frame can show posture, but a dynamic fault may require its timing and trajectory. Test temporal methods for those faults. [3]–[6]
  3. Separate fault detection from coaching. FormCoach demonstrates that actionable wording can be wrong. AIFit makes the source of a cue more traceable. Our language model should phrase supported detections, while its faithfulness is evaluated separately. [1] [4]
  4. Test the intended deployment. Restricted camera setups, small populations, and differing held-out tests limit transfer. Evaluate new people using the target phone and measure full correction delay, rather than adopting a published percentage as an expected result. [2] [4]–[6]

Limits of this review

The studies provide evidence about isometric holds, upper-body exercises, dynamic squats, and reference coaching. They do not establish our chest-press accuracy, a universal camera position, or reliable generated trainer anatomy. Those remain questions for Project 2.

Corrected skeletons and reference imagery in AIFit and the squat study offer concrete visual-feedback examples. They provide a more controlled baseline to evaluate before adding a generated demonstration. This is a design lesson, not proof that reference imagery is always correct. [4] [6]

Final conclusion

The strongest successes are specific: simple features work for some stable poses, temporal methods capture movement, reference methods localize deviations, and language models produce flexible wording. The weaknesses are also specific: restricted testing, uncertain geometry, reduced generalization, and unsupported corrections. Our project should combine these lessons and evaluate each stage honestly.
