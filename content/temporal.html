Comparison: classify a movement or match a reference?

Real-Time Posture Correction of Squat Exercise: A Deep Learning Approach for Performance Analysis and Error Correction learns seven squat types from calibrated pose sequences. Pose Trainer, AIFit, and SiTrEx instead use reference comparisons in different forms. All preserve information beyond one isolated frame. [6] [3] [4] [5]

What worked?

Pose Trainer's DTW handles unequal repetition speed instead of comparing same-index frames. Its reported average F1 is .85, 1.00, .85, and .73 for four exercises, but the corresponding test sets contain only 7, 12, 13, and 15 examples. The perfect score is therefore limited evidence of broad reliability. [3, §4.7; Table 1]

AIFit's estimated multi-view poses produce repetition-segmentation IoU .730, close to .731 using ground-truth 3D. At tolerance δ=.5, its feedback agrees about 80% with its own ground-truth-3D coach. This supports resistance to pose-estimation error under that evaluation, rather than independent trainer approval. [4, Table 2; Fig. 7]

The squat-correction study reports 96.10% classification accuracy for its Bi-CGRU with attention, compared with 94% for Bi-GRU with attention. That is a useful within-study architecture comparison. Stereo input and a split without established participant separation qualify its transfer to our phone application. [6, Table 2]

Where did performance fall?

SiTrEx's similarity model has 94.4% accuracy and 87.9 F1 in random-sample five-fold evaluation. With exercise types held out, accuracy falls to 81.5% and F1 to 59.8. This is direct evidence that its harder generalization task reduces performance. Reference matching helps expand beyond a fixed class list, but does not make new exercises equally easy. [5, Tables 6, 9]

Conclusion from the comparison

Classification names known categories; reference matching describes similarity or deviation from an example. The former depends on labeled classes, while the latter depends on the reference and alignment. For our project, neither should be selected from headline accuracy alone: compare them on the same users and clearly defined faults.
